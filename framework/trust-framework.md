# Marketing Copy Trustworthiness Framework

This framework is a **transparent heuristic** for reviewing AI-assisted marketing copy. It is not a legal standard, not an SEO score, and not an objective truth test. It helps humans spot common patterns that make copy *feel* less trustworthy.

## Why this matters

AI tools can produce fluent, confident marketing language quickly. That speed is useful, but fluent copy can still be vague, overhyped, or hard to verify. Growth marketing quality depends on human judgment: specificity, credibility, clarity, and honest framing.

---

## 1. Specificity

**Definition:** Does the copy describe a concrete situation, user, and outcome — or mostly abstract product language?

**Why it matters in marketing:** Specific copy helps readers understand what changes for them. Vague copy sounds like it could apply to anyone, which weakens relevance and recall.

**Weak example (illustrative):**
> Our innovative platform delivers a seamless workflow experience for modern teams.

**Stronger example (illustrative):**
> Help hiring teams screen candidates faster without spending hours on manual resume review.

---

## 2. Verifiability

**Definition:** Can a reasonable reader check or support the claim?

**Why it matters in marketing:** Unsupported stats and superlatives create credibility risk. Even true claims can backfire if they look invented.

**Weak example (illustrative):**
> Industry-leading AI proven to increase hiring success by 300%.

**Stronger example (illustrative):**
> Teams use this to cut first-pass resume review time — share your own benchmark in customer stories when you have data.

---

## 3. Clarity

**Definition:** Can a normal reader understand the message quickly?

**Why it matters in marketing:** Attention is short. If the value proposition takes too long to decode, the copy fails before the CTA.

**Weak example (illustrative):**
> We synergize next-generation talent optimization ecosystems across stakeholder workflows.

**Stronger example (illustrative):**
> See which applicants match your role requirements in minutes, not days.

---

## 4. Audience Awareness

**Definition:** Does the copy name who it is for and what problem they face?

**Why it matters in marketing:** Trust grows when readers recognize themselves. Generic copy feels like broadcast noise.

**Weak example (illustrative):**
> The ultimate solution for every business.

**Stronger example (illustrative):**
> Built for recruiters at fast-growing startups who need to move candidates through screening quickly.

---

## 5. Hype Control

**Definition:** Does the copy avoid exaggerated, sales-heavy language without substance?

**Why it matters in marketing:** Hype can create a short emotional spike but often reduces long-term trust, especially in B2B.

**Weak example (illustrative):**
> Revolutionary game-changing AI that will transform hiring forever.

**Stronger example (illustrative):**
> Automate repetitive screening steps so recruiters can focus on conversations with strong fits.

---

## 6. Logical Soundness

**Definition:** Do the claims make sense without overpromising universal outcomes?

**Why it matters in marketing:** Overbroad guarantees invite skepticism and can create compliance or brand risk.

**Weak example (illustrative):**
> Guaranteed hiring success for everyone, automatically.

**Stronger example (illustrative):**
> Helps teams prioritize qualified applicants — results still depend on your process and role requirements.

---

## Limitations

- **Subjective:** Trust is context-dependent. A bold claim might work in one channel and fail in another.
- **Keyword-based:** The prototype uses simple signals, not full semantic understanding.
- **Not legal advice:** This does not replace regulatory, legal, or brand review.
- **No source checking:** The tool does not verify whether statistics or citations are real.
- **English-focused:** Patterns are tuned for common English marketing phrases.

Use this framework as a **starting checklist**, then apply domain expertise, brand voice, and fact-checking before publishing.
